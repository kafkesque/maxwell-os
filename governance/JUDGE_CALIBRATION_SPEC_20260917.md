## Why this exists (finding F-10)

**R5/C8: the Generator may not be the Verifier — by FAMILY, not by model.** Six scripts judge or
classify objects that were generated by `Qwen3-Coder-30B` (Qwen family) while judging with
`Qwen3.8-27B` (Qwen family). Same family => shared training lineage and shared biases => **they
agree on each other's mistakes**, so the check passes and proves nothing. Every verdict those runs
produced is **uninformative** — not necessarily wrong, but worthless as evidence.

## Protocol (blind by construction)

The **human labels first, and sees no model output.** That ordering is the whole value of the
exercise: if the reviewer can see a proposal they will anchor on it, and the "calibration" then
measures obedience instead of accuracy.

1. Human fills `HUMAN_VERDICT` (+ optional confidence/note) in
   `governance/JUDGE_CALIBRATION_SHEET_20260917.csv` — **36 rows**, one question each.
   Do not open any model, and do not look at the prior `*_qwen38` artifacts.
2. Human writes `reviewer_date`.
3. *Then* the two candidate judges are run over the identical rows (a scripted ~72 calls, minutes).
4. Agreement is computed per task per judge against the human column.
5. Verdict per task is read off the thresholds in `config/eval_integrity.yaml::judge_calibration`.

## Decision rule (thresholds live in config, not here)

| measured accuracy | verdict |
|---|---|
| >= `min_accuracy_adopt` (0.85) | the judge may replace the same-family verifier outright |
| `min_accuracy_conditional` (0.70) .. 0.85 | usable, but record the error bar and escalate disagreements to a human |
| < 0.70 | the task must be made **deterministic** or become **human-owned** — do not paper over it with a weak judge |

If **both** candidates fall below 0.70 on a task, that task is a candidate for deterministic
re-implementation (the strongest outcome available: a verifier that cannot be wrong by opinion).
